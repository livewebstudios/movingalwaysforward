Ask most managers how their one-on-ones go and you will hear something like "fine, we go through the project list." Ask their direct reports and you will hear something closer to "it is a status meeting with one person in it."

A status update belongs in a shared document or a quick message. The one-on-one is something else entirely. It is the only meeting on the calendar built for one person, and it should belong to them.

**Let them set the agenda.** Before each session, ask them to send two or three things they want to talk about. Put their items first, always. Yours go at the end, if there is time. It makes clear whose meeting this is, and it teaches them to come in with something worth discussing.

**Ask questions that are not about tasks.** "What is getting in your way right now?" "What part of your work are you enjoying least?" "What should I be doing differently as your manager?" That last one is uncomfortable to ask. Ask it anyway, and do not argue with the answer. Say thank you and write it down.

**Talk less than they do.** Aim to speak for well under half the meeting. When they pause, wait a beat before filling the silence. The useful thing often comes out right after the pause, once they realize you are actually listening.

**Never cancel. Move it if you have to.** Canceling a one-on-one tells your report that everything else on your calendar matters more than they do. If something comes up, reschedule it the same week. Consistency is what makes people trust the meeting enough to bring real problems to it.

**Keep a running page for each person.** A simple shared page: what you talked about, what each of you agreed to do. Start every meeting by checking the last list. If you said you would look into something, this is where they find out whether you did.

Done well, the one-on-one becomes your early-warning system. Frustrations show up while they are still small. Career goals come up before someone starts quietly interviewing elsewhere. Problems you would never hear about in a team meeting get said in a room with two chairs and a closed door.

This week, send each person on your team a one-line message: "What do you want to cover in our next one-on-one?" Then let them drive.

*Building a team that tells you the truth is leadership work. That is what executive coaching is for.*
