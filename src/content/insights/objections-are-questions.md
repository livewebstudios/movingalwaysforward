"It is too expensive." "We are happy with what we have." "Now is not a good time."

Most salespeople hear those lines as a door closing. They either fold or push harder, and both moves make it worse. Folding tells the buyer the price was soft. Pushing tells them you were not listening.

Here is a more useful way to hear them. An objection is usually a question in a bad mood. "It is too expensive" often means "I do not see yet why this is worth it." "Now is not a good time" often means "I am not convinced this is urgent." The buyer is still talking to you. That is a good sign.

**Welcome it out loud.** Say something like "That is a fair concern. I am glad you raised it." You are not stalling. You are lowering the temperature, and you are showing that objections are safe to say in front of you. The ones that never get said are the ones that quietly kill deals.

**Ask what is underneath.** "Too expensive compared to what?" "What would make the timing right?" One honest question turns a vague objection into a specific one, and a specific objection is something you can work with. Sometimes the real issue turns out to be something else entirely.

**Agree with the part that is true.** If it does cost more than the other option, say so. "It does cost more up front." Then explain what the difference buys them. Denying the obvious costs you credibility you will need for the rest of the conversation.

**Answer once, then check.** Give a short, direct answer, then ask "Does that cover it, or is there more to it?" Do not stack five reasons on top of each other. A long defense sounds nervous, and it buries the one reason that mattered.

**Keep a list of the ones you hear most.** Most sellers hear the same handful of objections again and again. Write yours down, along with the best questions and answers you have found for each. Practice them out loud with a colleague. Objections lose their sting once you can see them coming.

A buyer who raises no objections is often a buyer who is not really considering it. The ones pushing back are the ones still thinking about saying yes. Treat their pushback as the question it is, and you stay in the conversation long enough to answer it.

This week, write down every objection you hear. By Friday you will have the start of your list.

*Objection handling is a skill teams can practice together. Build it into your next corporate training.*
