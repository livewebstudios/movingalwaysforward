You can tell how a meeting is going to go in the first two minutes. If it opens with "So, what did everyone want to talk about?" you already know. Forty-five minutes of updates, a tangent or two, and everyone leaves unsure whether anything was decided.

Bad meetings are not a personality problem. They are a design problem. And design is something you can fix before anyone walks into the room.

**Write the purpose as a sentence.** Not an agenda topic like "planning." A sentence that says what will be true when the meeting ends: "By the end of this meeting, we will have picked the two projects we are dropping." If you cannot write that sentence, you may not need a meeting. An email might do.

**Invite the people who decide, not everyone who is curious.** Every extra person makes a meeting slower and the decision softer. Invite the people who own the outcome and send notes to everyone else. People rarely resent being left out of a meeting as much as they resent being stuck in one.

**Cut it to three items and put them on the whiteboard.** Three things fit in anyone's head. Write them where everyone can see them. When the conversation drifts, nobody has to play the bad guy. Point at the board and ask which item this belongs to.

**Stop five minutes early to assign the work.** Use the last five minutes for one thing: who is doing what, by when. Say it out loud and write it down. A decision with no owner is just an opinion everyone agreed with for a minute.

**Send the notes within the hour.** Three lines are enough. What we decided, who owns each next step, and when we will check on it. It keeps everyone honest, and it means the people you did not invite still know what happened.

The best meetings are short, a little boring and extremely clear. People leave knowing exactly what to do next. Do that a few times in a row and something shifts. People start arriving on time and prepared, because they have learned your meetings actually go somewhere.

Look at your calendar for next week. Pick one meeting you run and write its purpose sentence today. If you cannot, cancel it and see whether anyone notices.

*Better meetings are a team habit, and corporate training is a good place to build it.*
