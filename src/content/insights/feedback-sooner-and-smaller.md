Most managers save feedback the way people save receipts. A little pile in a drawer, growing all quarter, until review time arrives and the whole pile gets dumped on the desk at once.

That is when feedback stops working. By then the moment is gone, the details are fuzzy, and the person across the table is hearing six months of notes in thirty minutes. They do not hear coaching. They hear a verdict.

The fix is simple and slightly uncomfortable: give it sooner, and give it smaller.

**Same week, ideally same day.** Feedback has a shelf life. The day after a meeting, "when you cut off the client during the pricing discussion" is a clear picture you both still remember. Three months later it is an argument about what happened. If it is worth saying, say it while the memory is still shared.

**One thing at a time.** Pick the single point that matters most and leave the rest for another day. People can change one behavior. Hand them five and they will change none, because they are too busy defending themselves against the list.

**Describe what you saw, not who they are.** "You are not detail-oriented" is a label, and people fight labels. "The numbers in Tuesday's report did not match the spreadsheet in two places" is a fact, and facts can be fixed. Stick to what a camera would have recorded.

**End with a question.** "What got in the way?" or "What would make this easier next time?" turns a correction into a conversation. Half the time you will learn something about the work you did not know. The other half, they will solve it themselves, which is the version that sticks.

**Do the same with the good stuff.** Positive feedback follows the same rules. "Nice job" evaporates by lunch. "The way you laid out the options before asking for a decision saved us twenty minutes" tells them exactly what to repeat. Specific praise is instruction, not flattery.

Small and soon has a side effect. Feedback stops being an event. When it shows up every week in two-minute doses, nobody braces for it. It becomes how the team talks, and the formal review turns into a recap of things everyone already knows. No surprises is the whole goal.

Start this week. Pick one person, one thing, and say it within a day. Then do it again next week, with someone else.

*Want feedback to become part of how your team runs? That is leadership coaching territory.*
