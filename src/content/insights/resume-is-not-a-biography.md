Most first resumes read like a life story. Every job since sixteen, every club, every course, a summary paragraph about being a passionate team player. It is all true. It is also why nobody calls.

A resume is not a record of your life. It is an ad for one specific job, read by a busy stranger who is looking for a reason to move on to the next one. Your only goal is to not give them that reason.

Here is how to cut it down to what works.

**Write it for the posting, not for you.** Open the job description and highlight the three things it keeps repeating. Those are the job. Now go through your resume line by line and ask whether each one proves one of those three things. If it does not, it goes. Yes, even the thing you are proud of. Sometimes especially that one.

**Replace duties with results.** "Responsible for social media accounts" tells them what was on your plate. "Grew turnout at club events by posting three times a week and tracking what worked" tells them what you did with it. Every bullet should start with a verb and end with something that changed because you were there.

**Kill the objective statement.** They know your objective. You want the job. Use that space at the top for two lines that say what you are good at and what you have done that proves it. If you cannot write those two lines, that is the real problem, and it is worth solving before you apply anywhere.

**Make it one page and make it skimmable.** Short bullets, consistent dates, plenty of white space. Hand it to a friend for ten seconds, take it back, and ask them who you are. If what they say is not what you wanted them to say, rewrite until it is.

**Keep a master file and tailor a copy.** Keep the long version somewhere safe, with everything in it. Then build a fresh one-page copy for every role that matters. It takes twenty minutes. Sending the same generic page to fifty postings feels productive and gets you about what you would expect.

The hard part is not formatting. It is deciding that some true, good things about you do not belong on this page. They are not wasted. They are interview stories. Save them for the room, where you get to tell them yourself.

A resume does not get you the job. It gets you the conversation. Write it for that.
