Your first ninety days have one job, and it is not brilliance. It is trust.

New hires hear "make an impact" and start hunting for the big win: the bold idea in the all-hands, the process nobody asked them to fix. Meanwhile the actual scoreboard, the one your manager keeps in their head, tracks something much more boring. Can I hand this person something and stop thinking about it?

That is the whole game early on. Be easy to manage. It sounds like a low bar. Watch how few people clear it.

Easy to manage looks like this:

**Close loops.** When you finish a task, say so. When you are blocked, say so early, with what you have already tried. A manager who has to ask "where are we on that?" is a manager doing your job's overhead for you, and noting it.

**Take notes in the open.** When someone explains something, write it down where they can see you doing it. You will ask fewer repeat questions, and the questions you do ask get better answers, because people invest in someone who keeps what they are given.

**Ask the second question.** "Got it" is how mistakes get made politely. "Got it, and when you say end of week, is that Thursday-ready-for-review or Friday-out-the-door?" is how trust gets built. Precision is not pestering.

**Bring the pen.** To every meeting. It signals you came to collect, not to perform. Twenty years from now this will still be the cheapest credibility you ever buy.

**Let them catch you being reliable.** Deliver small things exactly when you said you would, several times in a row. Reliability compounds faster than talent in the first year, because talent they hoped for. Reliability they cannot believe they got.

None of this is servility. You are not auditioning for a personality transplant. You are building the account you will draw on later, when you do have the bold idea, the pushback, the ask for more scope. People with trust in the bank get heard. People without it get managed.

Month four, the job changes. With trust established, you have earned the right to be a little harder to manage: to push, to question, to redirect. That is the career starting for real.

But it starts with the boring win. Be the person they stop worrying about.
