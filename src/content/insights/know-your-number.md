Ask a salesperson their quota and they will tell you instantly. Ask how many conversations they need this week to hit it and you usually get a pause, then a guess.

That pause is the problem. A target is a wish until you break it into things you can actually do on a Monday morning. Know your number, and the week stops being a vague hope and becomes a plan.

**Work backward from the goal.** Start with what you need to close this month. Then use your own history to figure out how many proposals it takes to get one yes, how many meetings it takes to get one proposal, and how many outreach attempts it takes to get one meeting. Say you need four deals and you usually close one of every three proposals. That is twelve proposals. Keep going down the chain until you reach the first activity.

**Use your real ratios, not the ones you wish you had.** Pull them from your own last few months, not from someone else's pipeline or a number you heard in a meeting. If you do not have the data, start tracking it now. A rough, honest ratio beats a precise fantasy every time.

**Turn the month into a week, and the week into a day.** Divide it out. If the chain says you need sixty outreach attempts this month, that is fifteen a week, three a day. Three is something you can do before lunch. Sixty is something you put off.

**Write it on paper where you can see it.** A notepad on the desk, not a report buried three clicks deep. Each morning, check off what you did yesterday and write what you will do today. Seeing the gap every day keeps you honest.

**Fix the weakest link first.** Once you track the chain, you will see where it leaks. Lots of meetings but few proposals? Your discovery questions need work. Plenty of proposals but few yeses? Look at pricing or how you follow through. The math tells you where to practice.

The best part of knowing your number is what it does to a bad week. Instead of a vague sense of falling behind, you know exactly how far behind you are and what it takes to catch up. That is a problem you can solve.

Do the math on Sunday night. Walk in Monday knowing exactly what the day requires.
