Every inbox has a graveyard. It is full of emails that were perfectly polite, reasonably important and never answered, because answering them would have taken real work.

The recipient opened it, saw four paragraphs, a buried question and three possible decisions, and thought "I will deal with this later." Later never came. That is not rudeness. That is triage.

The fix is to write the email they can answer in one line.

**Put the ask in the first sentence.** Not the background. Not "I hope this finds you well." The thing you need: "Can you approve the revised budget by Thursday?" Context goes underneath for anyone who wants it. If they only read the top line, they should still know exactly what you want from them.

**Make it a yes or no, or a choice.** "Thoughts?" is homework. "Option A or option B?" is a ten-second reply. Do the thinking for them. Lay out the choices, say which one you recommend and why, and let them pick. People answer decisions quickly. They postpone open-ended questions forever.

**One email, one ask.** If you need three things from someone, consider three separate emails, or at least number the questions. The second and third questions in a long email are the ones that never get answered, and you will not notice until the deadline.

**Give the default.** "If I do not hear back by Wednesday, I will go ahead with option A." This is not pushy. It is a kindness. It lets a busy person agree by doing nothing, and it keeps your work from stalling in someone else's inbox.

**Write a subject line that does the job.** "Quick question" tells them nothing. "Approval needed by Thursday: revised budget" tells them what, when and how much attention it needs before they even open it.

Before you hit send, read only your first two lines. Pretend that is all they will see, because on a phone between meetings, it might be. If those two lines do not contain the question, rewrite.

This takes a little more effort on your end. That is the point. Every minute you spend making the email easy to answer is a minute you save the reader, and readers notice who respects their time. Those are the emails they answer first.

Pick the most important email you need to send tomorrow. Write the ask first, then build everything else around it.

*Clear writing is a team skill, and it can be trained. Bring it into your next corporate training.*
