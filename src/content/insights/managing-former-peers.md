Last week you were grabbing lunch with them and complaining about the boss. This week you are the boss. Nobody hands you a manual for that, and the first month can feel strange for everyone involved.

The good news: the awkwardness is normal, and it passes faster if you deal with it head on. The bad news: if you avoid it, it does not go away. It just goes underground.

**Name it in the first week.** Sit down with each former peer, one at a time, and say what everyone is already thinking. "This is a little weird. I know it. I want to hear what you need from me, and I want to be honest about what changes." Saying it out loud takes the tension out of the room. Pretending nothing changed makes it worse.

**Stop venting with your old crew.** This is the hardest one. You can still be friendly, still eat lunch together, still care about each other. But the complaints about leadership, the gossip about other teams, the eye-rolling in meetings: you are leadership now. Every comment you make carries weight it did not carry before.

**Be visibly fair, especially to friends.** Your closest friend on the team gets the same standards, the same kind of assignments and the same feedback as everyone else. Everyone is watching that relationship more closely than any other. One perceived favor can undo a month of good work.

**Ask for help with what you do not know yet.** Your former peers know parts of the work you never touched. Ask them. "You know this process better than I do. What would you change?" It respects their experience, and it signals you are not trying to prove you know everything.

**Find someone outside the team to talk to.** You just lost the people you used to think out loud with. Replace them with a mentor, a fellow manager or a coach. You need somewhere to work through the hard calls that is not the team you now lead.

Some friendships will change. A few might cool off for a while. That is a real cost, and it is fine to feel it. But most people would rather have a fair boss they used to sit next to than a stranger. If you lead well, many of them will end up glad it was you.

The first month sets the tone. Have the uncomfortable conversations early, and the rest gets easier.
