Most managers think they delegate. What they actually do is dictate a checklist, then wonder why nobody on the team ever takes initiative.

If you tell someone exactly how to do a task, step by step, you have not handed them the work. You have handed them the typing. The thinking is still yours, which means the job is still yours, which means every question and every surprise comes right back to your desk.

Real delegation hands over the outcome.

**Describe what done looks like.** Before you assign anything, finish this sentence: "This is finished when..." A client deck the sales lead can present without edits. A vendor chosen and under contract by the fifteenth. Be specific about the result and say nothing yet about the method.

**Name the guardrails, then stop.** Every task has limits: budget, deadline, people who must be consulted, lines that cannot be crossed. Say them out loud. Inside those lines, the how belongs to them. Guardrails protect the business. Step-by-step instructions mostly protect your comfort.

**Agree on check-ins up front.** Delegating is not disappearing. Pick the moments you will look at progress, say a quick review at the halfway point, and stick to them. Then you are not hovering in between, and they are not guessing when you will show up.

**Expect a different route.** They will not do it the way you would. Sometimes their way will be slower. Sometimes it will be better, and you will be quietly annoyed you did not think of it. Judge the outcome you described, not the path they took to get there.

**Let the credit go with the work.** When it lands, their name goes on it. Out loud, in the meeting, in front of your own boss. Nothing builds an owner faster than seeing that the ownership is real.

The first time you do this, it will feel slower. You could have done it yourself by Tuesday. That is true, and it is the trap. Every task you keep is a skill nobody else on the team gets to build, and a ceiling on how much your team can carry.

So pick one thing this week that you would normally micromanage. Write down the outcome, the guardrails and the check-in date. Hand it over, then keep your hands in your pockets.

*Letting go is one of the hardest shifts a leader makes. Executive coaching can help you make it.*
