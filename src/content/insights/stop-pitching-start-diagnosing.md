Most sales calls go wrong in the first five minutes. The salesperson has a deck, a script and a quota, and the moment the small talk ends, the pitch begins. The buyer sits back, folds their arms and waits for it to be over.

Think about the last time a doctor skipped the questions and went straight to writing a prescription. You would not trust it, even if it happened to be right. Selling works the same way. Prescribing before you diagnose is malpractice, and buyers can feel it.

So flip the order. Diagnose first.

**Write your questions before the call, not your pitch.** Take a page and write down eight or ten questions you need answered before you could honestly recommend anything. What are they doing now? What is it costing them? Who else cares? What happens if nothing changes? Walk in with that page instead of a deck and the call goes somewhere different.

**Ask about the problem, then ask again.** The first answer is the polite one: "We want to improve efficiency." Follow it with "What does that look like on a bad day?" The second and third answers are where the real problem lives, usually in plain words, and usually with some emotion attached.

**Find out what it costs to do nothing.** A problem without a price is a nice-to-have. Ask what this is costing them in time, money, people or sleep. Let them do the math out loud. When the buyer names the cost, you never have to argue that it matters.

**Play it back before you recommend.** "So if I have this right, the real issue is X, it is costing you Y, and it has to be fixed before Z." Then stop talking. If they say yes, you have earned the right to propose. If they correct you, even better. You were about to pitch the wrong thing.

**Say so when it does not fit.** Sometimes the diagnosis shows you are not the right answer. Tell them. It costs you one deal and buys you a reputation, and the person you were honest with tends to remember your name when a real fit comes along.

When you diagnose well, the pitch gets short. You are no longer presenting everything you sell. You are connecting the two or three things that solve the problem they just described to you, in their own words.

And the buyer stops waiting for it to be over. They lean in, because now the conversation is about them.
